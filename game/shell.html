FORCE gathers what is in reach; the button becomes SHOOT until the wheel is empty. Single guides one stone into the crosshair and executes the wounded. Burst throws the lot — three landing together detonate a kinetic wave.

Strain builds as you use the power and recovers when you ease off. Burst costs about twice what an aimed shot does. Max it and you overload: reach and carry cut until it clears. Watch the bar.

Objects run out where you are standing. Fire everything nearby and you will read NO OBJECTS IN RANGE. They are still out there — move. Boulders and girders beat armour; barrels chain; chem burns.

FIRE throws one of the fireballs riding behind your shoulders. They grow back on their own, a few seconds each, up to the number your level allows — so your pressure is a clock, not a place. Every one detonates where it lands, so a miss into a crowd is still worth the shot.

There is nothing on the ground to pick up. That is the trade: you never have to go and fetch ammunition, and you never get a boulder when you need one. Beat a boss to raise your level for good — it is kept between runs, and it is the only thing here that is.

CUT throws one of the blades turning behind your shoulders. They gather again on their own, about two seconds each, up to the number your level allows — so your pressure is a clock, not a place. A blade does not burst: it cuts through up to three bodies in a line and keeps going, so where you stand decides how much one shot is worth.

There is nothing on the ground to pick up. That is the trade: you never have to go and fetch ammunition, and a shot into empty air is worth nothing at all. Beat a boss to raise your level for good — it is kept between runs, and it is the only thing here that is.

FLOW draws a ball of water up out of the ground, holds it a moment at head height, and throws it after one of them. It bends onto its target rather than flying where you pointed, so it is the one attack here that does not need to be led — and it lands hard enough to take a body off its feet and put it somewhere else. Aim is a suggestion; the shove is the point.

You carry nothing. There is nothing on the ground to pick up and nothing riding your back — the water is not there until you call it, and what you have is a number of times you can call it, which refills on its own. Beat a boss to raise that number for good: it is kept between runs, and it is the only thing here that is.

CAST is one button for the whole run, but not one attack. What it throws changes as you level: a plain bolt at first, a piercing shard once you have grown past it, a detonation once you have grown past that. The number held still refills on its own the way it does for the others — the difference is that the SHAPE of what refills is not fixed.

The level that changes it is permanent. A boss kill raises it for good, same as any other caster, and it is kept between runs. Reaching the next tier is the whole point of this one.

ARC hits one body and then jumps to the next, and the next, losing strength each time. Into one body it is worth less than a fireball; into five standing together it is worth far more. This is the only attack here whose damage depends on how the crowd is standing, so the question it asks is never "where is a target" but "where are they thickest".

SPIT barely hurts what it hits. What it leaves does: a pool on the ground that eats anything standing in it for several seconds. It is the only attack that damages GROUND rather than bodies — so you are not aiming at the crowd, you are aiming at where the crowd has to walk.

BIND is the weakest hit on the roster and that is the point. A struck body wades at a third speed for four seconds, and the shot cuts through two of them. It kills almost nothing on its own — it buys you distance, and it stacks a crowd up for whatever comes next.

SET does almost nothing when it lands. It sticks where it hits and counts down, swelling as it goes, and then goes off far harder than a fireball. The delay is the cost and the reason the blast can be that big — it is the one attack here you aim at where a body is going to be rather than where it is.

PULL barely scratches anything. It drags everything nearby into one point — the only attack here that gathers a crowd instead of scattering it. On its own it wins nothing. What it does is build the target every other kit wants.

BURST sends one shot out and four arrive: it breaks apart where it lands and fans fragments along the line it was already travelling. It covers width rather than a line or a point, which makes it the answer to a crowd that is spread thin — the opposite problem to the one the stormcaller solves.

DRAIN banks the damage you deal, and every full bank buys a heart back. Health here is five whole hearts, so it pays out in whole ones — you are working toward a heart, not watching a number creep. It is the only attack that gives anything back, and it is deliberately slow: roughly nine clean hits.

THROW sends it out, and at eighteen paces it turns and comes back — cutting on the way out and again on the way home, because it forgets what it already hit at the turn. It is the only attack dangerous in two directions, so its range is a decision rather than a limit.

PLACE is not aimed at anything. It lands, plants itself, and then fights on its own for eight seconds while you go somewhere else. Everything else here is thrown; this is put down. It refills slower than anything on the roster, because what it leaves keeps working after you have paid for it.

BLAST throws five at once in a cone, each one weak. The fan opens wider than a body past a few paces, so at range it is nearly useless — and at arm's length every pellet lands. It is the only attack here that wants the crowd close.

Every attack has a wind-up — a flaring ring and a sound. Step, dash or jump out of it and it whiffs. Grabbers root you; DASH always breaks it. Disruptors spike your strain from range. Kill them first.
